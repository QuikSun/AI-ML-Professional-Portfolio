Predicting Survey Panel Inactivity

Which active panel members might go the next 10 weeks without completing a survey? Follow one neural-network training step to see how historical outcomes can help answer that question.

Define the prediction

The research team wants to prioritize which members to check in with. Supervised learning fits this question because earlier records contain both a snapshot of member activity and the outcome that followed.

At the prediction date

Use information already available, such as how recently the member completed a survey and how often they responded to recent invitations.

Over the next 10 weeks

Assign label 1 if the member completes zero surveys and label 0 if they complete at least one. Use records with a full, observed follow-up window for this example.

See how the weight update works
  1. Forward pass. Scale the inputs to 0.50 (14 ÷ 28 days) and 0.80 (4 ÷ 5 invitations). The hidden neuron adds the weighted inputs and bias, giving (0.50 × 0.60) + (0.80 × 0.50) − 0.10 = 0.60. ReLU keeps this positive value. The output applies sigmoid to (1.20 × 0.60) − 0.70, producing the initial score of about 0.505.
  2. Loss and backpropagation. Binary cross-entropy measures how far the score is from the recorded outcome. Backpropagation calculates how changing the recency weight would change the loss. Here its gradient is (score − label) × 1.20 × 1 × 0.50. The 1 is the slope of ReLU at the positive input.
  3. Gradient descent. Subtract 0.80 times the gradient from the recency weight and run the same inputs through the network again. The demo holds the other parameters fixed so you can trace the effect of changing one weight.

Test the model before using it

Does the score improve on a simple rule?

Compare the model with a rule based on days since last completion. Use a later cohort held out from training. At the same review capacity, compare how many future inactive members each approach catches and how many of its flags are correct. Check whether flagged members received enough invitations to participate.

Does a check-in change participation?

Randomly assign eligible flagged members to a check-in or the usual contact schedule. Compare completions and opt-outs to see whether extra outreach increases participation and whether it increases opt-outs.

This case builds on a survey-panel practice question explored with the AI coach for 3.3 AI Training Interaction. The worked example applies the network training concepts from 3.2 Neural Networks.

How the training method changes with the question
Predict inactivity from confirmed outcomes
Supervised learning uses the 10-week outcomes as labels. A neural network is one model that can learn from those labels.
Discover patterns in engagement
Unsupervised learning can group members by activity when the team has no predefined group labels.
Learn a sequence of reminder decisions
Reinforcement learning would need a setting where an agent can choose actions and receive feedback, such as a simulation that rewards completions and penalizes opt-outs.
